# The Innovation Relay: Re-Ignite Event
## Detailed Agenda & Facilitation Guide

---

## Event Overview

**Event Name:** The Innovation Relay  
**Duration:** Full Day (8 hours)  
**Participants:** 25-50 people  
**Target Audience:** Cross-functional teams needing collaboration momentum  

**Purpose:** Break down silos and reignite cross-functional collaboration through rotating team challenges where groups build on each other's work.

**Unique Approach:** Instead of teams owning one idea from start to finish, the relay format forces collaboration and builds on collective intelligence. Each team inherits and improves previous work, creating investment across groups and preventing idea ownership battles.

## Hour-by-Hour Agenda

### 8:00 AM - 8:30 AM: Arrival & Breakfast
**Format:** Informal networking  
**Setup:** Coffee, breakfast spread, name tags with team colors  
**Music:** Upbeat, energizing playlist  

**Facilitator Notes:**
- Greet participants warmly

---

### 8:30 AM - 9:00 AM: Energy Ignition
**Objective:** Physical warm-up + demonstrate relay concept  
**Format:** Hands-on team challenge  

**Activity:**
1. Divide into 4 teams of 6-12 people
2. Each team gets 15 minutes to build something with LEGOs/materials (no instructions)
3. After 15 min, teams rotate clockwise
4. Next team has 15 min to IMPROVE what previous team built
5. Rotate once more
6. Final presentations showing evolution

**Debrief Questions:**
- How did it feel to inherit someone else's work?
- What made it easy/hard to build on previous work?
- What did you learn about collaboration?

**Materials:**
- 4 sets of building materials (LEGOs, blocks, or craft supplies)
- Timer visible to all
- Camera to document progression

---

### 9:00 AM - 9:45 AM: Challenge Marketplace
**Objective:** Surface real business problems; democratically select challenges to tackle  
**Format:** Interactive marketplace  

**Activity:**
1. **Setup (5 min):** Create 4-6 "booth" stations around room
2. **Present Challenges (20 min):** 
   - Department leaders or volunteers present 1-2 real challenges at each booth
   - Format: 3-minute pitch describing problem, impact, current barriers
3. **Marketplace Walk (10 min):**
   - Participants circulate, asking questions
   - Leaders clarify challenges, add sticky notes with details
4. **Voting (10 min):**
   - Each participant gets 3 tokens
   - Vote for challenges that excite them most
   - Can allocate multiple tokens to one challenge

**Output:** Top 4 challenges selected for Sprint Relay

**Facilitator Notes:**
- Coach challenge presenters beforehand: Be specific, make it real
- Ensure diversity of challenge types (operational, strategic, people, tech)
- If voting is lopsided, facilitate discussion to balance

**Materials:**
- Booth signs
- Challenge presentation templates (problem, impact, why it matters)
- Voting tokens (poker chips work well)
- Large vote tracking board

### 9:45 AM - 12:15 PM: Sprint Relay Rounds (2.5 hours)
**Objective:** Generate layered solutions through collaborative relay  
**Format:** 4 relay stations, teams rotate every 30 minutes  

#### Station Setup
**4 Stations, each with:**
- Top voted challenge displayed prominently
- Large workspace (whiteboard or poster boards)
- Materials: Markers, sticky notes, templates
- Timer (countdown visible)
- "Relay Instructions" poster

#### Round Structure (30 minutes each x 4 rounds = 2 hours)

**Round 1 (9:45-10:15):** Foundation
- Teams start at assigned station
- Goal: Define the problem deeply
- Output: Problem statement, root causes, stakeholder map
- Hand-off notes: What should next team focus on?

**10:15-10:25:** 10-min break + transition
- Teams rotate clockwise
- 2 min to review previous team's work
- Brief energizer/stretch

**Round 2 (10:25-10:55):** Ideation
- Inherit Round 1 work
- Goal: Generate solution ideas (quantity over quality)
- Output: 10-15 solution concepts building on problem analysis
- Add: Pros/cons, feasibility notes
- Hand-off: Which ideas have most potential?

**10:55-11:05:** Break + transition

**Round 3 (11:05-11:35):** Refinement
- Inherit Rounds 1 & 2
- Goal: Select top 3 ideas and detail them
- Output: Detailed solution designs with:
  * How it works
  * Resources needed
  * Risks and mitigations
  * Quick wins vs. long-term plays
- Hand-off: Implementation recommendations

**11:05-11:15:** Break + transition

**Round 4 (11:15-11:45):** Implementation Planning
- Inherit all previous work
- Goal: Create actionable roadmap
- Output:
  * 30-60-90 day plan
  * Owners for each phase
  * Success metrics
  * First 3 concrete steps

**11:45-12:15:** Buffer + Preparation for Showcase
- Teams review the full journey at their final station
- Prepare 5-min presentation
- Identify key contributors from each layer

**Facilitator Role:**
- Circulate constantly
- Keep energy high with music between rounds
- Call out when 15 min, 5 min, 1 min remain
- Document work in progress with photos
- Intervene if team gets stuck (ask questions, don't solve)

**Materials per Station:**
- Challenge description poster
- Relay instruction posters for each round
- Large whiteboard or 6+ poster boards
- Markers (multiple colors)
- Sticky notes (3+ colors)
- Dots stickers for voting/prioritizing
- "Handoff Template" for notes to next team
- Camera/tablet for documenting

**Facilitator Notes:**
- Push for SPECIFIC, MEASURABLE commitments
- Ensure names are attached to actions
- Get leaders to verbally commit resources

**Materials:**

- Markers

## Troubleshooting Guide

### Problem: Team stuck/frustrated during relay
**Solution:** 
- Ask guiding questions, don't give answers
- Remind them: Progress > Perfection
- Suggest breaking problem into smaller pieces
- Offer 5-min consult with another team

### Problem: One team dominating discussions
**Solution:**
- Use structured formats (round-robin sharing)
- Give quieter members specific roles
- Break into smaller groups

### Problem: Running behind schedule
**Solution:**
- Identify lowest-value activity to shorten
- Never cut relay rounds (core experience)
- Buffer is built into transitions

### Problem: Low energy mid-afternoon
**Solution:**
- Add physical energizer (jumping jacks, dance-off)
- Play upbeat music
- Change room setup/movement
- Share early wins/insights

### Problem: Executive feedback too negative
**Solution:**
- Frame as "constructive constraints"
- Ask execs to also identify what IS possible
- Remind: We're validating, not killing ideas

## Customization Options

### For Virtual/Hybrid
- Use breakout rooms for stations
- Miro/Mural becomes primary workspace
- Use digital timers and notifications
- Add more breaks for screen fatigue
- Ship physical materials to remote participants

### For Smaller Groups (10-20)
- Run 2-3 stations instead of 4
- Extend relay rounds to 45 min each
- Make it half-day (eliminate some sessions)

### For Larger Groups (50-100)
- Run 6-8 stations
- Use parallel tracks
- Add assistant facilitators
- Create "observer" role for some participants

### For Different Cultures
- Adjust ice-breakers to cultural norms
- Consider timing (some cultures prefer shorter days)
- Adapt competition/award style
- Modify food/beverage options

## Why This Format Works

1. **Collaboration By Design:** Relay structure forces teams to build on each other's work
2. **Reduced Ownership Battles:** No single team "owns" a solution
3. **Maintains Energy:** Physical movement, variety, clear milestones
4. **Real Outcomes:** Ends with commitments, not just ideas
5. **Builds Empathy:** Teams experience others' perspectives
6. **Creates Stories:** Memorable format people talk about
